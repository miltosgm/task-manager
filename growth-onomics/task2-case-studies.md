## CASE STUDY 1 · GGPOKER EUROPE · 35% Sign-Up Lift + 50% ROAS Improvement

**Client:** GGPoker — one of Europe's leading licensed online poker platforms, operating across Netherlands, Poland, Belgium, Germany, France and additional EU markets

**Services:** Google Ads Strategy · Compliance Architecture · Creative Framework · Retargeting Infrastructure · Attribution & Reporting

**Key Results:**
- +35% New Player Sign-Ups
- +50% ROAS
- +45% Event Participation
- +40% Retargeting CVR
- +30% CTR
- +25% Brand Recognition in Restricted Markets

---

### Situation

GGPoker had a genuine growth opportunity in Europe but was being blocked from capturing it — not by the product, and not by lack of budget, but by a marketing infrastructure problem masquerading as a regulatory problem.

Across five major European markets, GGPoker's Google Ads campaigns were either underperforming, regularly getting disapproved, or operating in a legal grey area that put their licence relationships at risk. The core issue: EU online gambling advertising operates under a patchwork of country-specific regulations — and what works in the Netherlands violates rules in Belgium, while France has entirely different restrictions again.

At the same time, GGPoker was trying to capitalise on major events like the World Series of Poker Online — where the acquisition window is short, competition is fierce, and every wasted impression is a lost player registration. Their retargeting infrastructure was underdeveloped, meaning players who visited the registration page but didn't complete were largely lost permanently.

**The business question:** How do you run effective, scalable paid acquisition for an online gaming brand across a fragmented regulatory environment — without sacrificing compliance or performance?

---

### Diagnosis

Three structural problems became clear after a systematic audit:

1. **The compliance architecture was missing.** There was no systematic process for tracking which creative assets, targeting parameters, and promotional mechanics were permitted in each jurisdiction. The team was operating reactively — modifying campaigns after disapprovals rather than building compliant infrastructure from the start. Each disapproval event cost days of impression volume and typically coincided with a competitor running uninterrupted.

2. **The targeting strategy was treating Europe as one audience.** The ideal GGPoker prospect in the Netherlands (poker-literate, competition-oriented, familiar with the brand) is a completely different person from the target prospect in Poland or Belgium (often new-to-poker, more risk-averse, responding to different messaging entirely).

3. **The retargeting funnel had a fundamental conversion leak.** Players who had visited the site — the most qualified, highest-intent audience segment — were being shown generic retargeting ads that didn't match the specific game type, event, or tournament they had shown interest in. The retargeting list segmentation was non-existent.

---

### Intervention

Growth-onomics built a five-layer intervention:

- **Compliance Matrix:** Built covering all five markets — permitted ad formats, targeting restrictions, content restrictions (bonus and incentive messaging rules per jurisdiction), and placement requirements. This became the foundation for every campaign decision.

- **Market-specific audience strategies:** Each market received a distinct targeting strategy with locally-compliant audience segments and tailored messaging — beginner-friendly in new-to-poker markets; competition and prestige messaging in established poker communities.

- **Modular creative library:** Legally pre-approved assets adaptable across markets without recreating from scratch. Compressed campaign launch time significantly during time-limited event windows.

- **Dynamic retargeting infrastructure:** Players segmented by game type interest, event registration intent, and deposit stage — shown ads specifically relevant to their demonstrated interest. Free tournament entries and time-limited bonuses used as conversion triggers for the highest-intent segment.

- **Measurement framework:** Google Analytics, GTM, and Looker Studio with campaign-level conversion tracking tied to first-time deposits (FTD) and tournament entries.

---

### Results

- New player sign-ups increased **35%** across target markets
- WSOP Online event participation grew **45%**
- Retargeting conversion rates improved **40%** (38% re-engagement among previously inactive registered users)
- ROAS improved **50%** overall
- CTR improved **30%**
- Brand recognition improved **25%** in markets where compliance restrictions prevented direct acquisition messaging

---

### Insight

What looks like a performance problem is usually a structural problem. More budget, better creatives, or different bidding strategies cannot fix a fundamentally non-compliant campaign architecture or an under-segmented retargeting infrastructure.

**Compliance architecture and marketing performance are not trade-offs — they are the same problem.** Operators who treat compliance as a constraint on marketing, rather than as infrastructure for marketing, consistently underperform operators who build compliant acquisition systems from the foundation up.

The retargeting insight: the highest-ROI audience segment in any gaming acquisition programme is almost always the registered-but-not-deposited pool. Most operators spend 80% of their budget acquiring this audience and 5% converting them. The inverse is the correct allocation.

## CASE STUDY 2 · FOREX BROKER JAPAN MARKET ENTRY · CPL Down 34% · ROAS Up 73%

**Client:** A major Forex/CFD broker based in Cyprus — established European operations with expansion ambition into Asian retail trading markets, specifically Japan

**Services:** Market Research & Intelligence · Facebook Ads (Japan) · Google Ads (JP, native-language) · Affiliate / IB Partnership Development · Multi-channel Attribution

**Key Results:**
- CPL: €29 → €19 (−34%)
- Avg. User Cost with Deposit: €298 → €143 (−52%)
- ROAS: 110% → 190% (+73%)
- Lead CVR: 4.5% → 8.6% (+91%)
- 39% of leads from affiliate · 25% from Google Ads

---

### Situation

A well-established Cyprus-based Forex broker had built a profitable European business over 10+ years. By 2022, ESMA leverage restrictions had compressed margins, competition from well-funded neobrokers was intensifying, and the cost of European customer acquisition had risen materially.

Japan presented a compelling alternative: the world's most active retail FX market by participation rate (13.2% of global daily Forex volume), with millions of active retail traders, high financial literacy, and an acceptance of FX trading as a mainstream financial activity. Average client lifetime value in Japan is materially higher than European equivalents.

But the broker's initial attempts to enter Japan independently had produced poor results. They were running English-language campaigns with European creative assets targeted at Japanese users.

---

### Diagnosis

Before proposing a single marketing action, Growth-onomics conducted structured market research across three dimensions:

- **Market Intelligence:** Japan's retail FX ecosystem operates on a distinct infrastructure from Europe. The JFSA (Japan Financial Services Agency) regulates all FX activity with strict rules designed to prevent Japanese retail investors from accessing unregulated offshore brokers. Any acquisition strategy that didn't account for JFSA compliance would reach the wrong audience.

- **The Rebate Channel Discovery:** Unlike European markets where performance is driven primarily by paid search and social, a significant segment of Japanese retail FX acquisition flows through dedicated rebate platforms — specialised comparison and cash-back sites (FXPlus, KaigaiFx, TozaiFx). Without representation on these channels, a new broker is effectively invisible to the most qualified segment of the market.

- **Cultural Communication Gap:** The existing creative assets were fundamentally misaligned with Japanese communication preferences. Japanese audiences respond to clear, concise, respectful communication — not the urgency-led, high-leverage performance language that works in European markets. The trust-building process is longer; the decision journey more deliberate.

- **Demographic Precision:** The primary retail FX trading demographic in Japan at the time was middle-aged men — a specific profile requiring precise targeting parameters.

---

### Intervention

**Channel 1 — Facebook Ads (Japanese Market):** Campaigns built around detailed demographic research, behavioural analysis, and income bracket data specific to the Japanese retail trading audience. Localised visual design and Japanese-language copy emphasising the broker's trading advantages through demonstration rather than assertion. Generated 42% lead conversion rate and 2.5% CTR — 35% of all qualified leads.

**Channel 2 — Google Ads (Japanese-Language):** SEMRUSH used to identify high-intent Japanese keyword searches (外国為替取引, 外国為替ブローカー, and related long-tail terms). Ad copy translated by fluent copywriters. Geo-targeting refined to high-density retail trading regions with dayparting aligned to peak trading hours. Generated 1.5M impressions, 15,000+ clicks, and 25% of total qualified leads.

**Channel 3 — IB / Affiliate Partnerships:** Growth-onomics identified and partnered with six leading Japanese FX rebate and affiliate platforms: Taritali, FXPlus, KaigaiFx, FxFan.club, TozaiFx, and GemgemFx. Generated 39% of all qualified leads at substantially lower cost per depositing trader than any paid channel.

---

### Results

- CPL compressed from $29 to $19 — **34% reduction**
- Average cost per depositing trader reduced from $298 to $143 — **52% improvement**
- ROAS improved from 110% to 190% — **73% improvement**
- Lead conversion rate improved from 4.5% to 8.6% — **91% improvement**
- Website traffic from Japan grew **56%** in the first 3 months
- Channel distribution: 39% affiliate, 35% Facebook, 25% Google Ads

---

### Insight

Market entry is not a campaign problem — it is a market understanding problem. In markets with established local intermediary ecosystems (Japan's rebate channels, South Korea's financial influencer networks, APAC's IB infrastructure), ignoring those channels and attempting to build purely through direct paid acquisition is both slower and more expensive.

**Culturally-adapted creative is not about translation — it's about a completely different communication strategy.** The performance differential between European-adapted content and genuinely Japanese-native content was not marginal; it was the difference between the campaign working and not working.

At the engagement level ($250K ad spend), a 73% ROAS improvement means the business is generating substantially more revenue from the same investment. That's the number that gets a CFO's attention and creates retainer renewals.

## CASE STUDY 3 · DATA-DRIVEN ACQUISITION RESTRUCTURE · 300% Revenue · 150% Acquisition · 3× Retention

**Client:** A growth-stage digital platform client with full access to their customer and channel data — 800 customer records spanning acquisition cost, channel, conversion rate, revenue, and CLV across paid advertising, email, referrals, and social media

**Services:** Marketing Data Analytics · Customer Segmentation · CLV Modelling · Channel Attribution Analysis · Budget Reallocation Strategy · CRM Integration

**Key Results:**
- +300% Revenue
- +150% Customer Acquisition
- 3× Customer Retention Rate
- Revealed Email as Highest-ROI Channel (despite lowest cost allocation)
- Identified Paid Advertising as Most Over-Invested Channel

---

### Situation

This client's problem was not lack of data — it was the opposite. They had accumulated 800 customer records across multiple marketing channels with data covering acquisition cost, channel source, conversion rate, revenue contribution, and retention metrics. But the data was being used for backward-looking reporting rather than forward-looking decision-making.

Paid advertising received the largest budget share (56.2% of total marketing spend) because it generated visible, trackable activity. Email marketing and referral programmes were systematically underfunded because their contribution was harder to attribute in the short term.

The mandate: use the existing data to identify the optimal marketing mix, quantify the opportunity cost of current allocation decisions, and provide a specific, implementable reallocation strategy.

---

### Diagnosis

Four analytical lenses revealed findings that directly contradicted the prevailing budget allocation logic:

1. **Profit Margin Analysis:** Subtracting total acquisition costs from revenue per channel revealed dramatically different margin profiles than the simple ROAS figures the client had been tracking.

2. **Customer Lifetime Value (CLV) Analysis:** By calculating CLV at the individual customer level and segmenting by acquisition channel, the analysis revealed a fundamental misalignment: the channel with the highest acquisition volume was producing customers with below-average CLV. The channel with the lowest volume was producing customers with above-average CLV — systematically.

3. **Channel Efficiency Modelling:** The 'cost per CLV unit' metric — total marketing cost divided by total CLV generated — stripped out the volume distortion. Paid advertising, despite generating the most leads, had the worst cost-per-CLV-unit performance. Email marketing, despite the lowest absolute spend, had the best.

4. **Conversion Rate Segmentation:** Social media had the highest average conversion rate (0.17) but the lowest total revenue. This apparently paradoxical finding — high conversion but low revenue — revealed that social media was efficiently converting low-value customers while email was converting high-value customers. The conversion rate metric alone was actively misleading the budget allocation process.

---

### Intervention

- **Customer Revenue Tier Segmentation:** Using quartile analysis, all 800 customers were assigned to four tiers (Low, Medium, High, Very High Revenue). Each tier analysed by acquisition channel to reveal which channels were disproportionately producing high-value customers.

- **Channel Reallocation Recommendation:** Reduce paid advertising budget share from 56.2% to approximately 35%; redirect freed budget to email marketing infrastructure and referral programme investment.

- **CRM Integration Requirement:** Implementing HubSpot with segmented nurture sequences as the operational change required to capture the full potential of the email channel.

- **Attributed Revenue Modelling:** Full attribution model built connecting marketing spend to customer revenue over a 12-month horizon, replacing the single-touch last-click attribution that had been creating the distorted channel picture.

---

### Results

- Revenue increased **300%**
- Customer acquisition increased **150%**
- Customer retention rate **tripled**

---

### Insight

Every business running multiple acquisition channels is, to some degree, allocating budget based on incomplete attribution. **Conversion rate and lead volume are not sufficient metrics for channel investment decisions.** A channel that converts many low-value customers at a low cost looks great on standard reporting and terrible on CLV-weighted analysis. Making budget decisions based on the wrong metric is not a performance failure — it is a measurement failure.

The practical application for regulated industry operators: CLV segmentation by acquisition channel, cost-per-CLV-unit efficiency modelling, and attribution over a 12-month customer horizon rather than a 30-day conversion window is what separates operators who grow profitably at scale from those who grow revenue while watching margins compress.

We run a version of this analysis as part of every Performance Audit engagement. The data requirements are modest (channel, cost, conversion, revenue, retention rate per cohort). The output regularly changes which channels clients invest in and which they cut.
